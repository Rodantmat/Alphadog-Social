The hotel in the middle of Zion Canyon sits on the bottom of a lake, a lake that vanished more than four thousand years ago. About 4,800 years ago, a huge part of the canyon wall near the Sentinel broke loose in about a minute, dammed the Virgin River, and left a lake that lasted roughly seven hundred years. In 1995 part of that ancient slide moved again and cut the only road in and out of the canyon.

In this episode: the Sentinel slide and the beryllium-10 dating that pinned it down; how a lake filled and then vanished under river sand and mud, leaving the flat floor of Zion Canyon; the lodge Union Pacific built on that lakebed, its 1966 fire and its 1990 restoration; the night of April 12, 1995, when the canyon became an island and crews carved a detour in 22 hours; why rocks fall in Zion, from Weeping Rock in 1968 to Cable Mountain in 2019; and what the deer, the cougars and the cottonwoods say about life on the old lakebed.

Filmed on the ground by us in November 2021 in Zion Canyon, at Angels Landing and in the Narrows.
