# Content-format research, 2026-11-25 (owner request: ground decisions in broad online research)

Evidence quality: most of these are vendor/blog analyses without primary data; where only one source says it, it is marked (1 src). Primary/official: YouTube policy pages & TechCrunch/Tubefilter/PPC Land coverage of the July 2026 clarification.

## What the sources agree on
| Question | Finding | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts length | Discovery sweet spot is short: median views peak at 11–20 s (108k-Short study); <10 s underperforms; >90 s drops sharply. Reels for new audiences <30 s. Story-driven (history/true crime) 30–50 s works only if retention holds. Our own data agrees (<30 s: 73% viewed vs 30–40 s: 60%). | wavevision.io, dataslayer (IG), easyviral, own YouTube data |
| Retention by length | Avg retention falls with length on every platform (TikTok 15–30 s 50–60%, 30–60 s 40–50%; Shorts/Reels 30–60 s 40–50%). Biggest drops: first 2–3 s and last 10%. | kinovela.com (aggregates RetentionRail/Retensis) |
| Visual change | Change the picture every ~2–5 s; scene >4 s needs a zoom/pan or a cut. A 30 s Short ≈ 8–12 scenes (1 src: 5,000-Short sample, retention 82% at 2–3 s changes vs 58% at 6+ s). | fluxnote.io (1 src, directional) |
| Hook | Three layers inside the first second (visual + on-screen text + audio); no greetings; numbered / curiosity-gap / mid-action / contrarian hooks; payoff held to the last ~5 s; end so the loop replays. | easyviral.ai, fluxnote.io |
| Narrative vs list | Narrative Shorts beat list formats; hook (1–3 s), build, payoff near the loop point. History/true-crime/animated storytelling are among the easiest niches to travel. | easyviral.ai |
| IG Reels signals | Watch time incl. replays, sends/shares per reach (strongest for non-followers), likes per reach, two-way conversation; originality/provenance (no watermarks, <10 reposts/30 d), audio required. | dataslayer.ai, eclincher, socialync (secondary; exact weights unverified) |
| YouTube | Viewer satisfaction + session contribution matter; Shorts and long-form are judged separately; Shorts watch-time-per-impression (~65% retention <30 s gets pushed, 1 src). | dataslayer.ai (secondary) |
| Motion on stills | Ken Burns: every move must reveal something (start tight→wide or wide→detail), slow and deliberate, hold frames so composition registers; constant zoom/pan "for its own sake" is the gimmick that reads as cheap. | poynter.org (Burns interviews) |
| Personal vs general | Viewers click on feelings: people/stakes, open loops, tension before payoffs, tone alternation, hook that restates the promise. No source measured personal-vs-general facts directly. | 1of10.com |
| Clips vs images | No source compares them head-to-head. Consensus: motion every few seconds; stills are fine if they move purposefully; real footage > stock > generic. | fluxnote, easyviral (gaps stated) |

## The risk that changes priorities (official, July 2026)
YouTube renamed/clarified "inauthentic content": three non-monetizable types — (1) generic/repetitive, template-like content incl. image slideshows and generic AI templates, little commentary or arc; (2) off-putting/emotionally manipulative formulas; (3) AI personas giving health/legal/finance/political advice. Enforced at channel level from a sample of recent uploads/most-viewed/most-watch-time, so a channel of near-identical faceless Shorts can lose monetization on everything. AI use itself is allowed; synthetic-realistic content must be disclosed. Survivors: niche educational channels, distinct videos, a recognizable voice, human input (TNW: some added on-camera hosts; the face-bias claim is 1 src).
Sources: tubefilter 2026-07-13, TechCrunch 2026-07-20, PPC Land, vidIQ, TheNextWeb.

## Decisions taken (no owner input needed)
1. Shorts 24–30 s incl. end card, Stories 15–25 s (already enforced).
2. Scene cadence 4–5 s: ≥5 distinct pictures per Short, ≥4 per Story where material allows.
3. Motion standard (MOTION=2 in build_shorts_p3.py): float sub-pixel crop (no integer stair-stepping), smoothstep ease, push-in/pull-out alternating toward the picture's detail centroid (Burns reveal), 0.5 s dissolves with both scenes moving, no hard cuts, no black borders; cuts land on caption boundaries.
4. Motion QA gate (tools/motion_qa.py) blocks the commit on hard cuts or freezes.
5. Against "template" flags: vary hook formulas and structure across a series (not one template), keep one story arc per video, every claim sourced in the factcheck file, keep `synthetic:true` disclosure.
6. Topic mix: keep narrative history anchored on specific people/places/dates (Grafton, CCC) — "personal" in the story sense (people with stakes), not first-person; science (geology) only as explainers tied to a place.
