September 14, 2015. Seven canyoneers enter Keyhole Canyon in Zion National Park. Barely more than half an inch of rain falls in about an hour, and the North Fork of the Virgin River goes from 55 to 2,630 cubic feet per second in fifteen minutes. None of the seven came out alive. That same afternoon, a flood in Hildale, just south of the park, killed thirteen women and children. This is the other side of one of America's most-visited canyons: the water and the rock that make Zion beautiful can turn deadly in minutes.

In this episode: why Zion's bare Navajo Sandstone turns every storm into a flash flood; the Narrows, where the trail is the river; the 1993 Kolob Creek tragedy and the six survivors who waited four nights on a ledge; the toxic cyanobacteria that killed a dog in 2020; Angels Landing, its chains, its deaths and the permit lottery; and how Zion went from fewer than 4,000 visitors in 1920 to more than five million.

Filmed on the ground by us in November 2021: the Narrows from the top down, Big Spring, Mystery Falls, Angels Landing and Zion Canyon.
