Dawn, August 24, 1877. A group of tourists from Montana is camped near Fountain Geyser, in Yellowstone's Lower Geyser Basin, when riders appear out of the steam. They are Nez Perce warriors. Behind them, crossing the world's first national park, come as many as 800 men, women and children with nearly 2,000 horses, running from the United States Army. This is the story of the 13 days when a war crossed Yellowstone (the Park Service says it may be the only war ever fought inside an existing national park), and of the people the Army was chasing: not invaders, but families trying to save their own lives.

In this episode: the nimíipuu and the 1863 "Thief Treaty" that cut their reservation by about 90 percent; Joseph (Hinmatóowyalahtq̓it) and the band that refused to give up its home in the Wallowa Valley; the dawn attack at the Big Hole; Poker Joe (Lean Elk), who led them through the park; the Cowan party, and George Cowan, shot and left for dead, who crawled about 12 miles over four days; the crossing of the Yellowstone River near Mud Volcano and the release of the captives; the escape past Colonel Sturgis to the Clarks Fork River; the five-day siege at Bear Paw, about 40 miles from Canada; the truth about "I will fight no more forever"; and the exile that followed.

Filmed on the ground by us in October 2021: Henrys Lake (Idaho), the Firehole River and the Lower Geyser Basin, Mud Volcano, the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone and Mammoth Hot Springs.
